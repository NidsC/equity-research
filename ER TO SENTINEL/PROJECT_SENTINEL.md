# SENTINEL — filings change-monitor for equity research

*Working codename. Rename freely.*

Written 2026-08-09.

---

## One paragraph

A standing system that watches a small watchlist of listed companies, notices when a new filing
lands, works out **what changed since the last one**, and tells you. On top of the accumulated
corpus sits a chat layer that can answer questions across every filing it has ever processed.
Financial figures come from XBRL and are computed in Python; the model's job is interpretation of
language, never arithmetic.

It is deliberately **not** a memo generator. Memo generation is a one-shot request/response
problem. Monitoring is a scheduled, stateful, partially-failing, long-running problem — which is
exactly the class of problem the diagnostic exposed as the gap.

---

## Why this one is feasible now

**No data collection phase.** SEC EDGAR is free, complete back to the 1990s, machine-readable, and
has structured XBRL financial data attached to every filing. There is no scraping project, no
licensing negotiation, no dataset to assemble before the first line of product code. The corpus
already exists and is public.

**The hard part is already solved and already yours.** `equity-research/` contains a working EDGAR
client with the rate-limiting rules implemented (`ingest/edgar.py`), section parsing
(`parse/sections.py`), and XBRL financial extraction (`parse/financials.py`). Lift them. Reusing
your own working code is not cheating; rewriting it is the waste.

**It starts useful at ten companies.** Unlike a product that needs scale to demonstrate anything,
this delivers value to exactly one user — you — on day one, at a token cost of pennies.

## Product thesis, with the reality check attached

**The thesis:** every investor holding a position has the same recurring, tedious job — a new 10-Q
or 10-K drops, and somewhere inside forty pages of largely boilerplate text, three sentences
changed in a way that matters. Risk factors gained a paragraph. Revenue recognition policy was
reworded. A segment was renamed. Litigation language hardened. Nobody reads both documents side by
side, so most of these are simply missed.

**The differentiator is the diff, not the summary.** "AI summarises filings" is a saturated claim —
every incumbent says it and most of them do it adequately. "Tell me the eleven things that changed
in this 10-K versus last year's, ranked by materiality, with the before-and-after text quoted" is a
narrower, more defensible, more obviously useful product. It's also a much easier engineering
problem to be *demonstrably correct* at, because the ground truth is right there in the two
documents.

**Second differentiator: the numbers boundary.** Figures come from XBRL, computed in Python, never
generated by the model. That means every number in the output is traceable to a filed value. This
is a real trust property in a category where hallucinated financials are the standing objection,
and you can state it as a guarantee rather than a hope.

### The honest market reality

This is a crowded category at both ends. AlphaSense, Bloomberg and Koyfin own the institutional
end. Simply Wall St, Fiscal.ai/Finchat and a long tail of newsletters own the retail end. Several
already do filings summarisation. You are not going to displace any of them.

What is genuinely underserved is **change detection on a personal watchlist for the serious
individual investor** — someone running 10–30 positions who reads filings but hasn't the time to
diff them. That's a real audience (r/SecurityAnalysis, UK value investing circles, part-time
analysts, students on CFA) and it's small enough that incumbents ignore it.

**Realistic outcome:** a handful of paying users at a low monthly price, or more likely a free tool
with a small following. Treat revenue as a bonus. Treat "does anyone else use it weekly" as the
real success metric. Do not build a business plan around it, and do not let it displace work that
already has users.

### Regulatory flag — read this before you take money

If you distribute this in the UK to anyone but yourself, keep the output **descriptive**, never
**advisory**. "The risk factors section added a paragraph about supplier concentration" is
description. "This makes the stock less attractive" is a personal recommendation, and personal
recommendations on securities are a regulated activity under FSMA — doing it without authorisation
is an offence, and financial promotions rules apply to how you market it.

Practical consequences: no buy/sell/hold language, no price targets, no "suitable for your
portfolio", clear disclaimers, and a settings default that keeps the model's tone factual.
Enforce it in the system prompt *and* check for it in your evals. This constraint is also, usefully,
a good engineering exercise — "does the output contain advisory language" is a testable property.

## Architecture

```
                    ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
   scheduled ──────►│  INGEST WORKER                       │
   (daily)          │  poll EDGAR per watchlist company    │
                    │  new filing → create job row         │
                    └──────────────┬───────────────────────┘
                                   │
                    ┌──────────────▼───────────────────────┐
                    │  PIPELINE (per document, resumable)  │
                    │   fetched → parsed → embedded →      │
                    │   diffed → triaged → analysed        │
                    │  each step idempotent, retryable     │
                    └──────────────┬───────────────────────┘
                                   │
              ┌────────────────────┼────────────────────┐
              ▼                    ▼                    ▼
      ┌───────────────┐   ┌─────────────────┐  ┌────────────────┐
      │ Postgres      │   │ REVIEW QUEUE    │  │ DIGEST         │
      │ + pgvector    │   │ (human gate on  │  │ weekly email   │
      │ filings,      │   │  material flags)│  │ what changed   │
      │ chunks, diffs │   └─────────────────┘  └────────────────┘
      └───────┬───────┘
              │
      ┌───────▼──────────────────────────────┐
      │ CHAT LAYER                           │
      │  hybrid search + safe SQL tool       │
      │  streaming responses                 │
      │  (same tools exposed over MCP)       │
      └──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Core tables

- `company` — ticker, CIK, name, added_at
- `filing` — company, form type, period, accession number, filed_at, source URL
- `document_job` — filing, `status`, `attempts`, `last_error`, `updated_at` — **the state machine**
- `section` — filing, section key (Item 1A, Item 7, …), text, hash
- `chunk` — section, text, embedding vector, token count
- `diff` — prior section, current section, change type, extracted delta text
- `finding` — diff, materiality score, model rationale, review status, reviewed_by

The `document_job` table is the heart of the learning. Status transitions are the only way work
progresses, every step is safe to re-run, and killing the process mid-run must lose nothing.

## Build phases

Ordering is deliberate: **observability comes after there is something worth observing, and evals
come after there is output worth grading.** That's the correction to the usual roadmap advice.

### Phase 1 — the spine. No AI at all. (~1 week)
Watchlist of 10 companies. EDGAR polling. `document_job` state machine with retries, exponential
backoff and idempotency. Section parsing and storage.

**Done when:** you can `kill -9` the worker mid-run, restart it, and end with no duplicates, no
lost work, and no re-fetching of completed steps.

This phase contains your single biggest gap and has nothing to do with language models.

### Phase 2 — the diff and the digest. First AI. (~1 week)
Structural diff of equivalent sections between consecutive filings. Cheap model triages each diff
for materiality. Weekly plain-text digest of what changed.

**Done when:** something lands in your inbox that you actually read.

That last sentence is the project's survival condition. Get here fast.

### Phase 3 — retrieval and chat. (~2 weeks)
Chunk, embed, store in pgvector. Build the chat layer with streaming responses.

**Deliberately start with naive vector search and let it fail.** Ask it "what was operating cash
flow in the most recent quarter" and watch it return prose about cash flow rather than the number.
Then fix it properly: hybrid search (Postgres full-text + vector, fused), metadata filters, a
reranker, and a separate structured-data path for anything numeric. Feeling the failure first is
the entire lesson.

The safe SQL tool lives here: read-only Postgres role, table allowlist, parameterised queries,
schema passed to the model, never string interpolation.

### Phase 4 — evals. (~4 days)
Hand-label 30 filing pairs: material change yes/no, and what it was. Score the pipeline on
precision and recall of material-change detection, plus a hallucination check (does any claim in
the output lack a quoted source in the filing?) and an advisory-language check.

Wire it into GitHub Actions on any PR touching prompts.

### Phase 5 — observability and cost. (~4 days)
Now there are ~40+ model calls per run and you cannot find the bad one by reading logs. Tracing
keyed by run and document. Cost per run, p95 latency per step, failure rate by step.

### Phase 6 — optional weekend
Wrap the query tools in an MCP server so Claude Code can interrogate your corpus while you develop.
One tool implementation, two surfaces.

## Cost

| Component | Choice | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Filings data | SEC EDGAR | free |
| Embeddings | `sentence-transformers` locally on the Mac | free |
| Triage model | Gemini Flash free tier, or Haiku | ~free |
| Analysis model | Sonnet/Opus on the ~10% that survives triage | pennies per run at 10 companies |
| Database | Neon or Supabase free tier (pgvector included) | free |
| Hosting | Render free/starter | free–£5/mo |
| Tracing | Langfuse cloud free tier | free |

**Realistic total: under £20 for the whole build.** Don't spend three weeks fighting free-tier rate
limits to save £15 — quota errors are not production errors and you learn nothing from them. Put
£20 of credit on an account and remove the friction.

## Risks and kill criteria

**Risk: it stalls, the way most side projects do.** The only defence is that Phase 2 puts
something in your inbox weekly.

> **Kill criterion — write it down and mean it.** If three weeks after Phase 2 ships you are not
> reading the digest, stop the project and say so. Noticing that early is worth more than finishing
> it, and a deliberate stop beats a repo that quietly rots.

**Risk: scope creep into a memo generator.** You already have one. If you find yourself building
memo generation, you're rebuilding equity-research and the learning stops.

**Risk: fifty companies on day one.** Ten. The learning is identical, the cost is a tenth, and the
runs finish while you're watching.

**Risk: UK/RNS coverage too early.** EDGAR is free, structured, and you have working code. UK
filings are a coverage feature, not a learning one. Later, if ever.

**Risk: regulatory.** Covered above. Descriptive, never advisory. Bake it into the evals.

## What it proves to an employer

Not "I built an AI app." Specifically:

- *"I run a scheduled pipeline over public filings. It's resumable — a failure at step four doesn't
  re-pay for steps one to three."*
- *"Naive vector search failed on numeric queries. I measured it, added hybrid retrieval and a
  reranker, and recall on my eval set went from X to Y."*
- *"I hand-labelled thirty filing pairs, so when I change a prompt I know within two minutes whether
  it got worse."*
- *"Financial figures are computed from XBRL in Python. The model never produces a number, because
  a model-generated figure is uncitable."*
- *"It costs Z pence per company per run, and here's the breakdown by step."*

Those are five sentences that most candidates for an applied AI role cannot say, and every one of
them is a measurement rather than a claim. That's the whole point of building it.
